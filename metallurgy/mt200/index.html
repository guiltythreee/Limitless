MT200 · 2-0-1 · 3 credits

Testing of Materials

Understand how engineering materials respond to mechanical loading, how those responses are measured, and how test results connect to material structure and properties.

5 modules4 practice papers4 model-solution sets
Course structure

Five modules

01

Introduction to Materials

  • Condensed matter to structural and functional engineering materials
  • Atomic, crystal, micro, macro and mega structures
  • Properties of metals, ceramics, polymers and composites
02

Tension and Compression Testing

  • Testing methodology, equipment, specimens and standard procedures
  • Engineering and true stress–strain curves
  • Strength, ductility, toughness, resilience and brittleness
03

Hardness Testing

  • Hardness and its correlation with strength
  • Scratch, rebound and indentation methods
  • Brinell, Vickers, Rockwell and microhardness tests
04

Miscellaneous Testing Methods

  • Torsion testing
  • Impact testing
  • Fatigue testing
  • Creep testing
05

Practical Exposure

  • Laboratory visits and demonstrations
  • Hands-on practice
  • Assignments

Course objectives

What this course develops

  1. Introduce structural engineering materials and their mechanical properties.
  2. Introduce mechanical testing methodology.
  3. Develop insight into structure–property correlations for metallic materials.
  4. Provide general practice and hands-on training in mechanical testing.
Learning outcomes

By the end of the course

  1. Understand fundamentals of structure–property correlations in engineering materials.
  2. Know mechanical testing methods, equipment and best practices.
  3. Develop numerical problem-solving skills in materials testing.
  4. Apply mechanical testing concepts in industry and research laboratories.

Evaluation in the supplied course plan: Lab 10% · Quiz 15% · Midterm 25% · End-semester 50%.

References: G. E. Dieter, Mechanical Metallurgy, McGraw Hill (1988); W. D. Callister, Materials Science and Engineering, Wiley-India (2009).
