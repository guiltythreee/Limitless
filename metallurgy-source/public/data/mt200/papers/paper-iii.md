## SECTION A · DERIVATIONS (44 marks)

A1.     Where modulus and theoretical strength come from. Two atoms interact through                                [7]
        U(r) = −A/rm + B/rn (n > m).
           (i) Find the equilibrium spacing r0 (in terms of A, B, m, n) and show that the bond
               stiffness S0 = (d2U/dr2) at r0 equals m·n·|U0|/r02, where U0 is the bond energy.
           (ii) Taking each atom to occupy an area r02, show that E ≈ S0/r0. What does this say
                about the link between E and melting point?
           (iii) Assume the stress needed to separate two atomic planes is σ = σth sin(2πx/λ). Using
                Hooke's law for small x and the requirement that the work of separation per unit area
                equals 2γ (γ = surface energy), show that σth = √(Eγ/r0).
           (iv) Evaluate σth for iron (E = 200 GPa, γ = 1 J/m2, r0 = 0.25 nm) and compare it with a
               typical yield strength of 250 MPa. What does the gap tell you?

A2.     Brinell versus Meyer hardness. Meyer hardness is the mean contact pressure, P divided                       [5]
        by the projected area of the impression. Show that BHN/Meyer = ½[1 + √(1 − (d/D)2)].
        Evaluate it at d/D = 0.25, 0.375 and 0.5, and explain which of the two is the more
        fundamental measure of the material's resistance to plastic flow.

A3.     Vickers geometry. For the 136° pyramid, show that the diagonal d ≈ 7.0 h, where h is                        [5]
        the depth of the impression. Hence find the projected area and the surface area in terms of
        h. Use your result to justify the usual rule that the specimen must be at least 1.5 d thick.

A4.     The friction hill in compression. A thin cylindrical disc (radius a, height h) is                           [6]
        compressed between rough platens with Coulomb friction (coefficient μ). Take the axial
        stress σz and radial stress σr (both compressive) with σz − σr = σ0, a constant flow stress.
           (i) Consider the radial force balance on a ring element and show that dσz/dr = −2μσz/h.
           (ii) Integrate with the free-edge condition at r = a to obtain σz(r).
           (iii) Find the mean pressure pavg and show that for small 2μa/h it is pavg ≈ σ0(1 +
                2μa/3h).
           (iv) What does this tell you about specimen proportions and lubrication?

A5.     Rebound hardness. A hammer is dropped from height h0 onto a specimen and rebounds                    [5]
        to hr. Using the coefficient of restitution e, show that hr = e2 h0 and find the fraction of the
        energy dissipated. Explain how this links to hardness, and why rebound height is not a pure
        measure of hardness.

A6.     Necking for a saturating hardening law. A metal follows the Voce law σT = σs − (σs −                 [5]
        σ0) exp(−εT/εc). Using the Considère criterion, derive the true stress and true strain at
        necking. Evaluate them, and the engineering UTS, for σ0 = 200 MPa, σs = 600 MPa, εc =
        0.5. Compare with the Hollomon law.

A7.     Machine compliance. A specimen (area A, gauge length L, modulus E) is tested in a                    [4]
        machine of stiffness km, with strain computed from crosshead displacement. Derive the
        apparent modulus Eapp. Evaluate it for a 10 mm diameter, 50 mm long steel specimen (E =
        200 GPa) in a machine of stiffness 100 kN/mm. Which measured quantities are corrupted,
        and which are not?

A8.     Meyer's law and strain hardening. Meyer's law states P = k dm. Assuming the mean                     [3]
        pressure equals a constant C times the flow stress at a representative strain εr = 0.2 d/D,
        and that σ = Kεn, show that m = 2 + n. What does m = 2 mean physically?

A9.     Error propagation in hardness. Show that the relative error in Vickers hardness is                   [4]
        δHV/HV = −2 δd/d. For Brinell, derive δBHN/BHN in terms of δd, D and d, and evaluate it for
        D = 10 mm, d = 4.0 mm, δd = 0.05 mm. Which measurement is more sensitive to reading
        errors, and why?


## SECTION B · HARD CONCEPTUAL AND THEORY QUESTIONS (32 marks)

B1.     Count the slip systems in FCC, BCC and HCP crystals. Explain why FCC metals are                      [4]
        inherently ductile, why BCC metals are strongly temperature-dependent in strength, and
        why HCP metals such as Zn and Mg are often brittle. State the von Mises requirement on
        independent slip systems.

B2.     Using the shape of the interatomic potential well, explain why materials with high melting           [4]
        points also tend to have high modulus and low thermal expansion. Contrast the moduli of
        steel, alumina and a polymer in terms of bonding.

B3.     Name the four main strengthening mechanisms in metals. For each, give the governing                  [4]
        relation and the physical origin of the strengthening. Which of them does not sacrifice
        toughness, and why?

B4.     List all the strength, stiffness and ductility parameters that a tensile test yields, and say        [4]
        how each is obtained from the curve. Compare elastic and plastic deformation at the
        atomic level. Why is yield strength, not UTS, the design parameter for ductile metals, and
        what does the yield ratio σy/σu tell you?

B5.     Why can no single stress–strain equation describe a tensile test past necking? Explain the           [4]
        stress state in the neck and the Bridgman correction (what must be measured). Describe
        the stages of cup-and-cone fracture.

B6.     Why is the compressive strength of cast iron, concrete or a ceramic several times its                [4]
        tensile strength? Explain using flaws, and explain why the fracture plane in compression is
        at about 45° or more, while in tension it is flat and perpendicular to the axis. Compare the
        method and outcomes of tension and compression tests for a ductile metal.

B7.     Explain why hardness is about 3 times the yield stress (the constraint idea) and why the             [4]
        flow stress relevant to it is that at a representative strain of about 8%. State two limitations
        of the H ≈ 3σ correlation. What are pile-up and sink-in, and how do they affect indentation
        results?

## SECTION C · NUMERICALS AND DATA ANALYSIS (24 marks)

C1.     Crystal-structure calculations.                                                                               [8]
          (a) Derive the atomic packing factor of FCC and BCC lattices.
          (b) Compute the theoretical density of copper (FCC, a = 0.3615 nm, atomic mass 63.55
              g/mol) and iron (BCC, a = 0.2866 nm, atomic mass 55.85 g/mol).
          (c) Compute the planar density of the FCC (111) plane of copper and of the BCC (110)
              plane of iron (atoms/nm2). Comment on what this says about slip.

C2.     Analysing tension data. The following engineering data were obtained in the plastic                           [8]
        region of a tension test on a metal (neglect elastic strain):

 Engineering strain (%)                2                  5               10           15               18
      Engineering stress (MPa)               358                   416         454           469              473

   (a) Convert each point to true stress and true strain.
   (b) By a log–log fit, find n and K in σT = KεTn.
   (c) Predict the true strain and engineering strain at necking, and the engineering UTS.
   (d) Estimate the energy absorbed per unit volume up to necking. Is the 18% point safely before
       necking?

C3.     Hardness calculations.                                                                                        [8]
          (a) Two Brinell tests (D = 10 mm) on a metal gave d = 3.50 mm at 1000 kgf and d = 4.80
              mm at 2000 kgf. Find BHN and Meyer hardness for each, check d/D, and find Meyer's
              index m and the strain-hardening exponent n.
          (b) A Vickers microhardness test at 100 gf gave diagonals of 48 µm and 50 µm. Find HV
              (in kgf/mm2). What is the uncertainty in HV from a ±1 µm reading error?
          (c) Find the depth (in mm) under the minor load for a hardened steel with HRC 55, and
              for brass with HRB 90 (use HR = 100 − e/0.002 mm for scale C and HR = 130 −
              e/0.002 mm for scale B). What depth difference corresponds to one Rockwell unit?
