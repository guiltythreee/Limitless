## SECTION A · DERIVATIONS (46 marks)

A1.     Derive the torsion formula $\dfrac{\tau}{r}=\dfrac{T}{J}=\dfrac{G\theta}{L}$ for a circular shaft. State the assumptions                     [6]
        clearly, and say why they hold only for circular sections. Evaluate J for a solid shaft and a
        hollow shaft, and obtain $\tau_{\max}=\dfrac{16T}{\pi d^3}$ for the solid case.

A2.     Show that $G=\dfrac{E}{2(1+\nu)}$. (Hint: a state of pure shear is equivalent to tension and                     [5]
        compression of equal magnitude on planes at 45°. Find the strain along the diagonal of a
        sheared square in two ways.)

A3.     For a bar under axial load P (area A), derive the normal stress and shear stress on a plane                 [5]
        whose normal makes an angle φ with the axis. Show that the maximum shear stress is σ/2
        at 45°. Then derive Schmid's law, $\tau_r=\sigma\cos\phi\cos\lambda$, and state the maximum Schmid factor
        and what it implies for slip in a single crystal.

A4.     Derive the energy absorbed in a Charpy test, $E=mgR(\cos\beta-\cos\alpha)$, from the pendulum                        [6]
        geometry, and obtain the striking velocity. Explain how the friction and windage loss is
        corrected, what the capacity of the machine is, and why the absorbed energy should not
        exceed about 80% of it.

A5.     Fatigue. (i) Define stress amplitude σa, mean stress σm, stress range Δσ and stress ratio R                 [6]
        in terms of σmax and σmin. (ii) Derive the Goodman relation $\dfrac{\sigma_a}{\sigma_e}+\dfrac{\sigma_m}{\sigma_{UTS}}=1$ (and with a
        safety factor n), and express the allowable σmax in terms of R. (iii) From two points on a
        straight-line log–log S–N curve, obtain the Basquin exponent b. (iv) State how Gerber and
        Soderberg lines differ from Goodman.

A6.     Creep. (i) Write the Arrhenius form of the steady-state creep rate. (ii) Show how the                       [6]
        activation energy Q follows from creep rates at two temperatures under the same stress.
        (iii) Using the Monkman–Grant observation (dε/dt)ss · tr = constant, derive the Larson–Miller
        parameter T(C + log tr), and explain how it is used and where it fails.

A7.     Derive the true fracture strain $\varepsilon_f=\ln\left(\dfrac{1}{1-RA}\right)$ from the percentage reduction of area,              [4]

        not (use Barba's law e = eu + β√A0/L0), and why %RA is the better measure of local

A8.     Write the generalised Hooke's law for a triaxial state. Derive the volumetric strain in terms           [4]

## SECTION B · HARD CONCEPTUAL AND THEORY QUESTIONS (32 marks)

B1.     Describe the five levels of structure (atomic, crystal, micro, macro, mega) and give one                [4]
        property governed at each level. Distinguish structure-sensitive from structure-insensitive
        properties, with examples, and show through two examples how the same composition can
        give very different properties.

B2.     Relate the bonding in metals, ceramics and polymers to their modulus, strength, ductility,              [4]
        density and thermal behaviour. Why is the stress–strain curve of rubber non-linear, and
        why does E of a polymer depend on time and temperature? What does a composite
        achieve that its constituents cannot?

B3.     Why does a tensile specimen have a reduced gauge section with fillets and shoulders?                    [4]
        What do standards such as ASTM E8 fix, and why? How does the choice of gauge length
        change the %EL you report, and what other test variables must be controlled?

B4.     Explain the yield-point phenomenon in low-carbon steel in terms of solute atmospheres                   [4]
        and Lüders bands. How is yield strength defined for a material without a sharp yield point?
        Distinguish proportional limit, elastic limit and yield strength.

B5.     Why is torsion testing used, and what advantages does it have over tension for studying                 [4]
        large plastic strains? Which specimen types are used and why must they be circular?
        Explain, from the stress state, why ductile metals fail in torsion on a plane perpendicular to
        the axis while brittle materials fail on a 45° helical surface.

B6.     What does an impact test signify? Compare Charpy and Izod tests (support, notch position,               [4]
        striking point). Discuss the factors that shift the ductile-to-brittle transition temperature
        (DBTT) and the criteria used to define it. Why do FCC metals show no DBTT?

B7.     Describe the three stages of a fatigue failure and the fracture-surface features that identify          [4]
        each. How do stress raisers, surface finish, residual stress and corrosion affect fatigue life?
        Distinguish endurance limit from fatigue strength, and high-cycle from low-cycle fatigue.

B8.     Explain the mechanisms of creep (dislocation, diffusional and grain-boundary sliding). Why              [4]
        is creep significant above about 0.4 Tm? Explain the three stages of a creep curve, and
        describe how turbine-blade alloys are designed to resist creep.
