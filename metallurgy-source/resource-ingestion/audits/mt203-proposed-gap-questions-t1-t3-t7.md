## T1 — lecture SBR composition trend

**Proposed home:** T1.5 — Copolymer architectures & thermoplastic elastomers. The question tests the lecture's SBR copolymer-property example, not nomenclature.  
**Type / difficulty:** concept / level 2  
**Source:** p.16

**Question**

The lecture compares styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) with homopolymers and marks styrene-derived content as the plastic-like component. According to the lecture, what trend is shown as styrene content increases?

A. Tensile strength decreases, while elongation and abrasion resistance increase.  
B. Tensile strength increases, while elongation and abrasion resistance decrease.  
C. Tensile strength and elongation both increase, while abrasion resistance is unchanged.  
D. Tensile strength is unchanged, while elongation and abrasion resistance both decrease.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The p.16 slide states that increasing styrene (plastic) content increases tensile strength, while elongation and abrasion resistance decrease. This is the specific SBR trend shown by the lecture.

**Distractor rationales**
- A: Reverses all three directions.
- C: Gives the wrong direction for elongation and claims no change in abrasion resistance.
- D: Misses the stated increase in tensile strength.

**Quality note:** The item tests the exact three-property trend rather than asking only what SBR is made from (already assessed in T8).

## T3 — solution-polymerization product isolation

**Proposed home:** T3.2 (solution polymerization)  
**Type / difficulty:** concept / level 2  
**Source:** pp.22–23

**Question**

After solution polymerization, which pair of routes does the lecture give for isolating the polymer from the solution, and what difficulty does it identify?

A. Filtration or water washing; the main difficulty is removing protective colloids.  
B. Solvent evaporation or precipitation in a non-solvent; removing the final traces of solvent is difficult.  
C. Centrifugation or bead filtration; the main difficulty is controlling monomer-droplet size.  
D. Melt extrusion or pelletization; the main difficulty is preventing micelle formation.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The lecture gives two isolation routes: evaporation of the solvent or precipitation in a non-solvent. It specifically notes that removal of the final traces of solvent is extremely difficult (p.23).

**Distractor rationales**
- A: Water washing and protective-colloid removal are associated with the suspension-process discussion, not the stated solution-polymer isolation routes.
- C: Monomer-droplet size is part of the suspension-polymerization discussion; these are not the two routes listed for solution polymerization.
- D: Melt extrusion/pelletization and micelle formation are not the solution-polymer isolation routes stated on pp.22–23.

**Quality note:** The item distinguishes solution isolation from suspension bead washing and emulsion micelle formation already covered elsewhere.

## T3 — surfactant classes, interfacial role and CMC

**Proposed home:** T3.3 (emulsion polymerization)  
**Type / difficulty:** apply / level 3  
**Source:** pp.28–30

**Question**

A student is checking a description of the lecture's emulsion-polymerization system. Which statement is consistent with the lecture?

A. Anionic surfactants include alkali salts of fatty acids and sulfonic acids; cationic surfactants include alkyl amine hydrochlorides and alkyl ammonium halides; non-ionic surfactants include alkyl glycosides and sucrose esters of fatty acids. Surfactants lower monomer–water interfacial tension, and above the critical micelle concentration (CMC) excess surfactant forms micelles.  
B. The three classes are as in A, but surfactants raise monomer–water interfacial tension; micelles form only below the CMC.  
C. Anionic surfactants are alkyl ammonium halides, cationic surfactants are alkali salts of fatty acids, and non-ionic surfactants are persulfate initiators; the CMC is the concentration at which all surfactant precipitates.  
D. Surfactants lower monomer–water interfacial tension, but micelles form only when the surfactant concentration is below the CMC; their polar ends point inward and hydrocarbon chains point outward.

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** The lecture lists the three surfactant classes and examples on p.28. It says surfactants lower surface tension at the monomer–water interface. On pp.29–30, it defines the CMC as the concentration beyond which micelle formation occurs and describes micelles with polar ends outward and hydrocarbon chains directed inward.

**Distractor rationales**
- B: Swaps the lecture's anionic and cationic example groups.
- C: Reverses the stated role of surfactants at the monomer–water interface.
- D: Reverses the CMC condition and the orientation of emulsifier molecules in the micelle.

**Quality note:** This combines the lecture's class examples with the functional and CMC concepts in one integrated item. If the course assessment requires finer granularity, split it into a class-matching item and a separate micelle-structure item rather than making this item longer.

## T7 — recombinant spider-silk production sequence

**Proposed home:** T7.3 — Collagen, keratin, fibroin, gelatin. The lecture's spider-silk example is a fibroin-related protein example; keep it within the existing protein-materials subtopic rather than inventing a new taxonomy node.  
**Type / difficulty:** sequence / level 3  
**Source:** pp.78–79

**Question**

The lecture's diagram for recombinant spider-silk protein production shows a sequence from spider genetic information to a protein product. Which order matches the diagram?

A. Spider genetic information → *E. coli* → plasmid with genetic information → fermentation → protein.  
B. Spider genetic information → plasmid with genetic information → *E. coli* → fermentation → protein.  
C. Plasmid with genetic information → spider genetic information → fermentation → *E. coli* → protein.  
D. Spider genetic information → plasmid with genetic information → fermentation → protein → *E. coli*.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The p.79 diagram shows spider genetic information leading to a plasmid containing genetic information, then *E. coli*, followed by fermentation and the protein product. The p.78 slide identifies the example as recombinant spider-silk protein C16/ADF4 (45 kDa), comprising 16 repeats of a C16 module with the printed sequence GTSAGTSAGTSA.

**Distractor rationales**
- A: Places *E. coli* before the plasmid stage, reversing the diagram's order.
- C: Reverses the first two stages and places fermentation before the *E. coli* stage.
- D: Places fermentation and protein production before the *E. coli* stage.

**Quality note:** The question tests only the labelled sequence. It does not infer specific molecular biology procedures absent from the diagram. The named protein details can be used in a separate item if more granular assessment is desired.
